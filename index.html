Welcome to the anonymous self-test on Lecture V: Northern Hemisphere Glaciation!

The lecture explores the onset and intensification of northern hemisphere glaciation (NHG) during the Late Pliocene, which results in distinct glacial–interglacial cycles of bipolar glaciation during the Pleistocene. It introduces the geological evidence for the development and expansion of northern hemisphere ice sheets by focusing on ice-rafted debris in marine sediments from the North Atlantic, the North Pacific and the Arctic Ocean. You should be familiar with what ice-rafted detritus is, what it tells us about the temporal evolution of northern-hemisphere ice sheets, and why it is a useful indicator for studying temporal and regional ice sheet changes.

The lecture also examines the proposed mechanisms responsible for permanent glaciation in the northern hemisphere, including orbital forcing, ocean gateways, tectonic uplift, atmospheric CO₂ changes and tropical ocean reorganization. You should be able to briefly describe and critically evaluate the discussed causes of NHG. Emphasis will also be put on numerical climate and ice-sheet model evidence on the proposed mechanisms, in particular possible interactions between these processes. From these discussions, you should have a sense of how modelling helps assess the relative importance of different mechanisms and feedbacks associated with NHG.

Literature recommendation: Ruddiman, W (2014) Earth’s Climate: Past and Future, 3rd edition, WH Freeman — Chapters 4–7.
